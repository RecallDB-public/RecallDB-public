How many recalls the U.S. issues

Across the five federal agencies, the United States issued an average of 7,338 recalls a year from 2012 to 2025, about 20.1 a day. The peak year was 2015 with 8,850; 2025 closed at 6,709, and 2026 stands at 4,194 through Sep.

Which agency issues the recalls

The FDA accounts for 67.8% of all recall records, NHTSA for 23.5% and CPSC for 6.4%. Food-safety recalls from FSIS are the smallest feed at 1.0%; 67.9% of their classified hazards are microbial contamination.

What the recalls are for

Drug defect is the largest hazard class at 32.0% of classified hazard assignments, followed by Crash injury (18.6%) and Microbial contamination (18.4%). 13.8% of assignments are not yet mapped to the taxonomy and are excluded from the shares.

How serious FDA recalls are

21.0% of the 87,314 classified FDA recalls are Class I, the most serious category. The Class I share peaked at 32.6% in 2023 and was 22.0% in 2025.

How many units a recall touches

NHTSA campaigns report the number of vehicles affected: 1,388,707,778 vehicle-units across 30,267 campaigns, a median of 681 per campaign, with 7.1% of campaigns above 100,000 units and 252 above one million. CPSC recalls report 667,991,265 units across 8,286 recalls, median 7,045. The largest single recall on record is a CPSC action from 2016 covering 118,000,000 units.

Which vehicle model years get recalled most

Model year 2021 carries the most recall entries, 6,663, of the 162,074 NHTSA make-model-year entries with a model year. Recent model years are still accumulating recalls, so 2026 and 2025 will keep rising.

When recalls are announced

May is the busiest recall month with 10,537 recalls over the period, 1.67 times Jan, the quietest. Friday is the busiest weekday; only 2.5% of recalls are dated on a weekend.

Firms with the most recall records

Ford Motor Company has the most recall records on file with 1,906, ahead of General Motors, LLC (1,787) and Chrysler (FCA US, LLC) (1,586). Vehicle makers lead because NHTSA files one campaign per defect across a whole fleet; medical-device makers follow because FDA files one entry per affected lot.

Method, reuse and citation

  • Source. The full RecallDB snapshot of 2026-09-18: 128,933 recall records pulled from the CPSC SaferProducts API, NHTSA's recall flat file, openFDA enforcement reports plus FDA Safety Alerts, the USDA FSIS recall API and the USCG recall list. All five are U.S. federal publications in the public domain. Every row keeps the agency's own ID, record URL, retrieval timestamp and raw-payload fingerprint; see Sources.
  • Nothing is estimated. Units, classifications, dates and firm names are the agency's own values; where an agency publishes no value the field is blank and excluded from the figure's denominator, which every section states.
  • Agencies are not directly comparable. One NHTSA campaign covers a fleet; FDA files one entry per lot; CPSC one notice per product. Counts describe records, not distinct safety events.
  • Refresh. RecallDB is refreshed on a manual cadence; this page and its charts are regenerated with each snapshot, so figures move. Cite the snapshot date. NHTSA has flagged 210 campaigns as do-not-drive and 104 as park-outside since 2012.
  • Reuse. The figures and charts on this page are published under CC BY 4.0: use them in articles, slides and posts with a link to https://recalldb.dataengineered.io/stats/. The machine-readable version is data.json. The underlying row-level ledger is a separate commercial product; a free 200-row sample is in the public repository.
